# Beagle Bros — the facts

The factual anchor for the skill. Keep the sensibility attached to what the
company actually made and said; do not invent products, slogans, people, or
business history.

This account was checked against original Beagle Bros catalogs, bulletins, and
manuals on 2026-09-14. Catalog descriptions establish that a product existed and
how Beagle Bros marketed it; they do not independently establish popularity,
quality, or commercial success.

## The company

- **Beagle Bros Micro Software** began in San Diego, California, in **1980**.
  Bert Kersey's signed introduction to the company's 1984 catalog calls October
  1, 1984 its fourth anniversary and describes the original operation as Kersey,
  his dog Sophie, a 16K Apple II, a back bedroom, and a quarter-page ad. The same
  catalog identifies Kersey as president of **Beagle Bros, Inc.**
- The company's catalogs centered on **Apple II software**. Their compatibility
  tables cover the II, II Plus, IIe, IIc, and, in later catalogs, the IIgs. Avoid
  turning that focus into an absolute claim that the company never published for
  another platform.
- A contemporary 1987 report says Bert and Sharon Kersey, described there as the
  husband-and-wife founders, sold Beagle Bros to former Beagle programmer Mark
  Simonsen. It reports that Beagle Bros and Software Touch, which Simonsen and
  Alan Bird had founded, were being combined. Beagle's 1988 catalog names **Mark
  Simonsen** the new "Head Beagle" and **Bert Kersey** the founder.
- Beagle's own 1988 summary says its emphasis evolved from games to utilities to
  AppleWorks enhancements. It also says almost every Beagle programmer was then
  concentrating on the **TimeOut** line. That supports calling TimeOut the
  company's later focus, but not the unsourced superlative "big commercial
  success."
- A 1992 interview with former employee Morgan Davis says Beagle sold its Apple II
  products to **Quality Computers**. That evidence supports a transfer of the
  Apple II product line; it does **not** support saying that the entire business
  and the TimeOut line were sold together.

The original sources reviewed do not explain the origin of the name or establish
that "there were no actual brothers." Treat the fictional brothers and dogs in
the catalogs as house humor, not documentary company history.

## Products confirmed in original catalogs and manuals

These are real titles, with names and functions normalized to the forms used in
Beagle's catalogs:

- **DOS Boss** — a DOS 3.3 enhancer by Bert Kersey and Jack Cassidy. It renamed
  DOS commands and error messages, customized catalog headings, and could make an
  unauthorized save attempt produce a "Not Copyable" message.
- **Pronto-DOS** — a DOS 3.3 speed-up and DOS mover. Beagle advertised it as
  roughly three times normal DOS speed; describe that as the company's claim, not
  an independently benchmarked fact.
- **Apple Mechanic** — a hi-res drawing, shape-table, font, and disk-inspection
  toolkit.
- **G.P.L.E. (Global Program Line Editor)** — Neil Konzen's Applesoft line editor.
  Beagle's July 1982 bulletin explicitly listed it as a non-Beagle product sold by
  mail through Beagle. Its December 1983 bulletin then announced that Beagle had
  become its sole publisher. Preserve that chronology rather than implying Beagle
  created it.
- **Beagle BASIC** — Applesoft enhancements, including added commands and the
  ability to rename commands and error messages.
- **Utility City** — a collection of 21 Applesoft utilities.
- **Alpha Plot** — a hi-res drawing and labeling program by Bert Kersey and Jack
  Cassidy.
- **Frame-Up** — a presentation program for sequenced hi-res, lo-res, and text
  frames; Beagle called it an Apple "slide projector."
- **Silicon Salad** — a collection assembled by Mark Simonsen and Bert Kersey from
  programs, utilities, two-liners, and tips.
- **Tip Disk #1** — 100 tip programs on disk.
- Other catalog-confirmed titles include **Flex Type**, **Triple-Dump**, **Big U**,
  **DiskQuik** (an extended-memory disk-drive emulator), **D Code**, **FatCat**,
  **I. O. Silver**, **Beagle Graphics**, and **Beagle Compiler**.
- Beagle's 1988 catalog lists these AppleWorks enhancements under the **TimeOut**
  banner: **DeskTools**, **DeskTools II**, **FileMaster**, **Graph**,
  **MacroTools**, **MacroTools II**, **PowerPack**, **QuickSpell**,
  **SideSpread**, **SpreadTools**, **SuperFonts**, **Thesaurus**, and
  **UltraMacros**.

If a title is not in a primary artifact, check it before adding it here.

## Documented house practices

- **Instruction mixed with jokes.** The catalogs and manuals visibly combine
  operating instructions, Apple tips, cartoons, comic asides, and promotional
  copy. Calling this "documentation as entertainment" is a fair description of
  the artifacts. Claims about how often customers kept or reread the manuals need
  separate evidence.
- **Peeks, Pokes & Pointers.** Beagle repeatedly included and sold its Apple
  memory-reference chart alongside software and tip books. The chart itself
  survives in the document archive. "Famous" is interpretation, not a fact the
  primary material can prove.
- **Copyable software.** The 1983 catalog's product index says "Copy-protected
  programs: NONE," and the 1984 catalog describes the company's approach as
  unprotected software users could learn from. Some earlier products or
  third-party disks were protected, so apply this description to the documented
  period rather than to every disk Beagle ever handled.
- **Old-style graphic design.** The catalogs visibly use engraved illustrations,
  ornate display type, pointing hands, decorative borders, and absurd captions.
  "Victorian mail-order catalog" is a useful modern description of that look, not
  Beagle's documented name for it.
- **Humor inside the programs.** Original catalog copy documents listable demos,
  joke programs, two-liners, custom comic error messages, and playful utilities.
  The broader claim that Beagle routinely hid "Easter eggs" in its software was
  not established by the primary sources reviewed, so do not present it as fact.

## One-line summary

Beagle Bros made compact Apple II tools and AppleWorks enhancements, then taught
people how to use them through unusually playful catalogs, manuals, tips, and
reference charts.
